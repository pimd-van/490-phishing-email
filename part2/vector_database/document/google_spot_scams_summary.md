# Google: How to spot scams, and what to do if you encounter one — original summary

This file is an original, source-grounded summary of Google’s October 2, 2024 article, not a reproduction of the copyrighted article. It is the exact article corresponding to the project document’s shorthand description “How to spot email, phone, text and web scams.”

Google advises treating unexpected messages and urgent requests for sensitive or financial information with suspicion. For email, verify the actual sender address rather than trusting its display name, watch for lookalike domains, inspect link destinations before clicking, and ignore password-reset messages you did not request. Spelling or formatting errors can still be warning signs, although polished language does not prove a message is safe.

For calls and texts, ask questions without volunteering information, avoid links and app downloads sent by unknown people, and never share temporary passwords, passcodes, or two-step verification information. Urgency is a warning sign. Use built-in spam warnings, but independently verify a caller or sender through contact information obtained from an official source.

On the web, inspect unfamiliar URLs before opening them, investigate advertisers and businesses using authoritative sources, and be cautious with unusual formatting or unofficial customer-service details. If scammed, change passwords, check whether account details were altered, contact banks and linked accounts, enable stronger protections such as passkeys and two-step verification, and report the crime to the appropriate authorities.
