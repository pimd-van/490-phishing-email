# Why these knowledge-base sources (rationale with evidence)

Written 2026-10-23, updated 2026-10-24. Eleven sources, 20 chunks.

## 1. How sources were selected (rules fixed independently of the email dataset)
1. **Authority**: an official body (government, standards agency) or a major platform vendor.
2. **Audience**: written for ordinary users, not administrators or developers.
3. **Actionable**: gives steps the reader can take.
4. **Verifiable**: has a URL and a date.
5. **Reusable**: terms allow paraphrase (see `sources.json`).

The "evidence" column holds external statistics that show the **topic** of each source matters. It does not prove the page is the best possible source, and no number comes from our own email sample.

## 3. Coverage and gaps
By number of reports, FTC 2024 ranked imposter scams, online shopping, business/job opportunities, then investment and internet services; by money lost, investment ($5.7 billion), imposter ($2.95 billion), business/job ($750.6 million) ([FTC, Mar 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/03/new-ftc-data-show-big-jump-reported-losses-fraud-125-billion-2024)).
- Covered: imposters, investment, job/business, prizes, medicine offers, spam, links, attachments, urgency, aftermath, reporting.
- **Not covered (state in the thesis):** online shopping and counterfeit goods, loans, business email compromise, extortion. Romance and inheritance approaches were added as STR-01 on 2026-10-24 (section 5); advance-fee contract proposals are covered by STR-01 only by analogy.
- Medicine has its own two chunks because of health-safety risk and distinct official guidance, not because of current frequency.

## 4. Limits of the evidence
- Figures are self-reported or vendor-reported, mostly US/UK, and FTC figures are not specific to email. They show relevance, not rates for any email corpus.
- Several figures come from news summaries (CBS, Axios, HIPAA Journal); cite the primary report before formal use.
- Copyright pages of FTC, NIST, FDA, NCSC and Microsoft were read on 2026-10-24; the S07 URL was matched to its title. The NCSC phishing page itself was not re-read for content.

## 5. Update 2026-10-24: S10, S11 and the changes to the chunk list
- **Added STR-01 (S10, S11).** FBI IC3 2025 annual report ([ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf), figures as returned by the reading tool; check the table in the PDF before citing): Confidence/Romance 23,159 complaints and $929,287,469; Advanced Fee 7,762 complaints and $155,910,852; Phishing/Spoofing 191,561 complaints and $215,843,126; total 1,008,597 complaints and $20.877 billion. Romance plus advance fee lost about 5.0 times the phishing/spoofing total with about 16% as many complaints (about $40,100, $20,100 and $1,100 per complaint). FTC reported $3.5 billion lost to imposter scams in 2025, nearly three times the 2020 level ([FTC, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/06/ftc-data-show-people-reported-losing-3-point-5-billion-imposter-scams-2025)). Limits: US complaints, not email-specific, under-reported; in our 100-row sample only 2 rows (about 2%) fit STR-01.
- **S10** (romance): scammers build trust, claim they are far away, ask for money for travel, medical costs or fees, favour wire transfers, gift cards, money apps and cryptocurrency, and press for speed; advice is never to send money or gifts to someone not met in person. **S11** (inheritance): the FTC says such letters are scams and the money does not exist; do not respond or send money or information, and report it. S11 is about mailed letters; STR-01 generalises the pattern.
- **AFT-01 and AFT-02 (old) merged** into AFT-01 to free a slot: the two chunks gave the same actions (change the password, recover the account, scan the device). Old AFT-03 (lost money) is now AFT-02, text unchanged.
- **Kept S03 (Microsoft)** for BRD-02, LNK-01, ATT-01, URG-01 and AFT-02 by the author's decision. In the 100-row gold set 66 rows carry at least one of these chunks (LNK-01 alone 61), so removing them would require relabeling.
- **Reuse terms read on 2026-10-24:** FTC (public domain for most site material; attribution requested), FDA (public domain unless noted), NIST (public unless marked copyrighted; credit requested), NCSC (Crown copyright under OGL v3.0; attribution and OGL link required; third-party images and logos excluded), Microsoft (personal, non-commercial use of Documents without modification; support.microsoft.com not named). FBI IC3 site: no copyright statement found; only numbers are cited.
- **MED-02 header changed** on 2026-10-24 from "Checking an online pharmacy before using it, and reporting a bad one" to "Email from an online pharmacy inviting you to order medicine", so that, like the other headers, it describes the email rather than the reader's action. Text, source and labels unchanged; re-embed MED-02.
- **Finding:** SPM-02 states that scammers use unsubscribe links to confirm an address is active. Neither S07 nor S02 says so as read on 2026-10-24. The author decided on 2026-10-24 to remove the clause; the chunk now ends after the advice not to click anything, including an unsubscribe link.
