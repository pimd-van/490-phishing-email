## Text

> If a message says you have won a prize, lottery or sweepstakes and asks you to pay taxes, shipping, processing or any other fee first, treat it as a scam: a genuine prize does not require payment. Scammers also rush you, so pressure to claim it today is part of the trick. Do not send money, give card or bank details, or reply. If you wonder whether the contest is real, look up the organisation's contact details yourself instead of using those in the message. Delete the message and report it to your country's consumer-fraud reporting service.

## When to assign (labeling rules v1.5)

- **Assign when:** The reader has already "won", been "selected" or been "approved" for money, a prize or a free item, or a fee is needed to receive money or a prize
- **Do not assign when:** Ordinary discounts and sales; an invitation to enter a contest; a "free gift" offered inside an advert
