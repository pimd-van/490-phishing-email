## Text

> Phishing emails often tell a believable story: suspicious activity on your account, a billing problem, a refund or coupon, an unpaid invoice, or a request to confirm your details. Ask yourself whether you actually have an account with this company and whether you know the sender. If not, do not click, reply or open attachments; delete or report it. If you do use the company, do not use the links or phone numbers in the message. Find its contact details yourself, from a statement, a card or by typing the official address, and ask whether the problem exists.

## When to assign (labeling rules v1.5)

- **Assign when:** The email tells a story about an account, order, delivery, billing, refund or record problem from a company the reader may not use
- **Do not assign when:** Plain advertising with no claim about the reader's account or order
